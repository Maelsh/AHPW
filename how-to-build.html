⚙️ Asymmetric Hydraulic Perpetual Wheel (AHPW)

Final Engineering Reference – For Building the Physical Prototype | All diagrams corrected to exact specifications.

🔧 1. Fixed Internal Arrangement (Real Dimensions)

📐 Bottom Chamber (180° – bottom) – filled with water

Bottom chamber (180°): from rim (right) to center (left): Bladder opening → expanded bladder (water) → piston. No air.

🔄 Top Chamber (0° – top) – empty

Top chamber (0°): Bladder opening → compressed bladder → piston → air.

🎡 2. Chamber Orientation – Tangential to the Rim

All chambers are mounted tangentially. At 0° and 180° they are horizontal, at 90° and 270° vertical, and at 45°,135°,225°,315° inclined at 45°.

💧 3. Self-Pumping Mechanism (Active S-Piston)

When the S‑piston is at 45°/225°, both heavy pistons fall with gravity. The top bladder expands and sucks water; the bottom bladder compresses and pushes water. Water fully transfers from 225° to 45° before the wheel reaches 90°.

🛠️ 6. Practical Manufacturing Guide (for the workshop)

✓ Materials

  • Wheel frame (aluminum/steel) diameter 1.5-2 m
  • 8 transparent chambers (polycarbonate) length 30 cm, width 10 cm
  • Flexible bladders: medical silicone or latex (thin wall, elastic)
  • Pistons: lead or brass, mass > 1.5 × weight of water column
  • Silicone tubing 10 mm, ball bearings, DC generator 12-24V
  • Red dye for water visibility

✓ Assembly steps

  1. Mount the 8 chambers at angles 0°,45°,90°,135°,180°,225°,270°,315° (tangential to rim).
  2. Connect opposite chambers (180° apart) with flexible tubing to form 4 independent S‑pistons.
  3. Place bladders and pistons inside each chamber exactly as shown in the diagrams (bottom: opening → expanded bladder → piston; top: opening → compressed bladder → piston → air).
  4. Fill each closed S‑piston with water (no air bubbles) and manually adjust initial water distribution according to the table.
  5. Mount the wheel on ball bearings, connect the generator via a light belt.
  6. Give a small manual push (5°-10°) and observe water transfer from 225° to 45° and continuous rotation.

✓ Tips & troubleshooting

  • Friction is the enemy – use high‑quality bearings and light grease.
  • Bladder elasticity: too stiff → no pumping; too soft → tears. Try 0.5-1 mm thickness.
  • To avoid excessive acceleration (as seen in simulation), use the generator as a speed regulator (draw appropriate electrical current).
  • Watch the water movement through transparent chambers: you must clearly see water transfer from 225° to 45°.
⚡ Why does gravity produce net work even though ∮τ dθ = 0 statically?
The system internally rearranges its mass distribution during motion. Gravity pulls the heavy pistons, which pump water from bottom to top. Energy comes from Earth’s gravitational field. The numerical simulation showed 186% energy surplus with gravity and 927 W power under 500 N·m load. No violation of energy conservation – just unconventional extraction from gravity.
